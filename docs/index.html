Transfer readiness check

Product discovery: moving an investment account between Canadian firms · October 2026

Self-initiated concept; public data; not affiliated with any institution. Pilot of 59 public posts; tags AI-suggested, not individually re-checked by me.

Made by directing AI agents: they collected and tagged the posts, drafted the analysis, documents and prototype, and reviewed each other's work; I set the goal and constraints and approved the problem choice and the final deliverables.

Problem

In 59 public posts, 7 (11.9%) describe a rejected request or form error, all at the handoff between firms (step 4); 4 were still stuck when posted. Each re-filing restarts the wait: “Re did the paper work. Rejected again.” (P003, 2018)

What I did

Collected 59 public posts with AI help (27 forum, 31 review-site, 1 app-store; no Reddit), so bad experiences are over-represented. Mapped the journey in 8 steps; 5 hypotheses were frozen before any post was read. AI suggested the tags; a blind AI re-tag of 20 rows agreed on pain 19 times and on step 15.

What I found

In 59 public posts, steps 4 and 5 hold 36 (61.0%). The three biggest step-by-pain cells:

  • Waiting × Slow: 14 (23.7%)
  • Request between firms × Rejected or form error: 7 (11.9%)
  • Fees and refunds × Fee surprise: 6 (10.2%)

Of 5 hypotheses, 2 were confirmed (one by a single row), 1 wrong, 2 unclear. The posts show that failures exist, not how often: there is no count of uneventful transfers.

The decision and why

Chosen: rejections at the handoff (step 4), as a receiving-firm product: a digital, pre-checked request that the sending firm accepts first time.

  • Controllable: the receiving firm starts the request and owns the form.
  • Measurable: first-time acceptance rate (first-pass yield).
  • Compounding: each rejection restarts the wait, so fewer should shorten part of the slow cell.
  • Fit: Lean right-first-time and error-proofing, applied to a service handoff.

Not chosen, in 59 public posts: A, slow processing (14): the fix appears to sit mostly with the sending firm. Notes suggest the sending firm in 9 of the 14 elapsed-time rows (my reading; review pages are organised by the firm reviewed). That a sending firm has little reason to speed departures is an assumption, untested. B, no visible status (4): too thin alone, so I folded in its “show the reason and the fix”. D, transfer-out fees (6): clearer causes and partly controllable (the expected fee is surfaced in R4), but fee levels sit with the sending firm.

Trade-off: A is twice as frequent as C in 59 public posts (14 vs 7). This is accepted, but C’s evidence is thin and must be sized from a firm’s data before any build.

What I’d build

A pre-checked, digital request for a fictional firm, Maple Invest; try the clickable prototype. MVP must-haves (RRSP and TFSA, in-kind and cash), each tied to a post:

  • R1 Check sending-account details before submission (P003)
  • R2 Block account-type and transfer-kind combinations the rules disallow, and offer an alternative (P015)
  • R3–R4 Warn about holdings the firm cannot hold, and about cash left for the old firm’s fee (P044, P043)
  • R5 Complete and e-sign online; no paper to lose (P056, P059)
  • R6 Rejection reason in plain words and a one-step fix, within 1 business day (P003, P029, P043)
  • R7 Status at the handoff: Submitted, Accepted, or Rejected: action needed (P018, P050)

Six Must stories (S1–S6) carry Given/When/Then criteria. Later: rejection log and auto-routing (Should), chase alerts (Could). Out of scope: sending-firm speed, fees, status after acceptance, account locks.

How I’d measure it

Set before designing. Baselines come from a firm’s 12 months of logs; targets are assumptions to validate, not commitments.

What I’d do next

  • Confirm or correct all 59 tags myself, then re-run every count here.
  • Interview 5 recent movers (at least 2 rejected); test the prototype with 5 people (pass: completed unaided).
  • With a firm’s 12 months of transfer-in data, apply a decision rule (assumed): if under a third of rejections are preventable at intake, revisit A or B before building.

Biggest risk: the assumption that many rejections are preventable at intake is unproven; in 59 public posts, 3 of the 7 give no cause.
